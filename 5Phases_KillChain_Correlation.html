5 Phases of Hacking  ↔  Cyber Kill Chain

How attacker methodology maps to Lockheed Martin's Cyber Kill Chain framework
5 Phases of Hacking
Cyber Kill Chain (Lockheed Martin)
Phase 1
Reconnaissance
Gathering information about the target — IP ranges, employees, technologies, open ports, social media, public records, DNS records.
⟶
Step 1
Reconnaissance
Attacker researches the target using OSINT, WHOIS, Shodan, LinkedIn, job postings, and passive scanning to identify entry points.
Phase 2
Scanning & Enumeration
Active probing of the target — port scans (Nmap), vulnerability scans, service enumeration, OS fingerprinting, banner grabbing.
⟶
Step 2
Weaponization
Attacker uses scan results to identify specific vulnerabilities and creates or selects an exploit — malware, payload, phishing lure — tailored to the target's environment.
Phase 3
Gaining Access
Exploiting identified vulnerabilities to break in — phishing, SQL injection, buffer overflow, credential stuffing, exploiting unpatched CVEs.
⟶
Step 3
Delivery
Weapon is delivered to the target — phishing email, malicious USB, watering hole attack, or direct network exploit.
Step 4
Exploitation
The exploit executes — code runs, vulnerability is triggered, attacker achieves initial foothold inside the target system.
Phase 4
Maintaining Access
Establishing persistent presence — backdoors, rootkits, scheduled tasks, new admin accounts, C2 beaconing, lateral movement to other systems.
⟶
Step 5
Installation
Malware or backdoor installed for persistent access — RATs, web shells, rootkits dropped onto the system to survive reboots.
Step 6
Command & Control (C2)
Attacker establishes remote control channel — beaconing to external server, encrypted tunnels, DNS exfiltration to receive commands and move laterally.
Phase 5
Covering Tracks
Erasing evidence of the attack — clearing logs, deleting files, timestomping, disabling audit trails, removing malware after data is stolen.
⟶
Step 7
Actions on Objectives
Attacker achieves their goal — data exfiltration, ransomware deployment, destruction, espionage — then removes traces to avoid detection and maintain long-term access.

Key Takeaways for Students
  • The 5 Phases describe the attacker's methodology — how they think and plan an attack from start to finish.
  • The Cyber Kill Chain describes the attacker's actions — the specific technical steps executed at each stage.
  • Phases 3 and 4 of hacking each map to two Kill Chain steps — gaining access requires both Delivery and Exploitation; maintaining access requires both Installation and C2.
  • Defenders use the Kill Chain to identify which step to break — stopping Delivery prevents Exploitation; blocking C2 limits the damage of Installation.
  • Both frameworks together give a complete picture: WHY attackers do what they do (5 Phases) and HOW they do it (Kill Chain).
